## Why the factor cap is 4

The generator's outer IDs are 0,3,…189. Each direction has a fixed Fibonacci latitude/azimuth plus a bounded 0–.08 rad azimuth perturbation, and a native speed from 13 to 19 m/s. Evaluate the midpoint directions on a 128-by-256 spherical normal net. For every net normal, retain only pairs whose projected unit-vector dot product is at most −.2; the largest eligible projected separation is at least 1.9595448508. A sphere normal lies within π/128 of a net normal. The actual direction differs from its midpoint by at most δ=2 sin(.02).

The projected dot-product perturbation is at most 2δ+sin(π/128)=.104536, so the chosen pair remains opposed in the projection. Its projected distance is therefore monotone in both positive radial speeds, and their minimum 13 m/s is safe. Deducting the projection and direction perturbations gives unit separation at least 1.8304627987. At drag 9.81/16 and age 2.5, the native width is consequently at least 30.4306653 m, giving alpha at most 3.943390616. This closes the cap4/76m/s generator envelope for arbitrary seed jitter and common 3D rotations. It is an analytic real-arithmetic enclosure supported by a finite deterministic grid and a generous numerical margin, not a formally directed-rounding floating-point certificate.

All outer reference profiles pass the fixed 10% own-peak gate at 2.5 s. At the shortest allowed lifetime, 3.196 s, the authored normalized Y is still at least .414804. Using the pre-I0 burn reference retains this geometric definition at intensityScale0; it does not make a dark firework visible.
