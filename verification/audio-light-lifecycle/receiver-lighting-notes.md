# Firework receiver lighting diagnosis

Production uses up to **16 firework diffuse-shader proxies** on the island's opaque MeshStandardMaterial receivers, plus three ground lamps and the flashlight: 20 shared local-source records/uniform slots in total. Only the first **four are native scene lights**. Slots 4–19 have detached, invisible THREE.PointLight compatibility mirrors but are not registered with the scene; their shader injection adds Lambertian direct diffuse and foliage thin-surface transmission, not native firework specular. The luminous ledger is unchanged. Every proxy retains summed current source candela, RGB and intensity-weighted centroid. Nine island receiver positions and directional irradiance components select spatial subdivisions; they do not fit or amplify output RGB. The full-current ledger supplies launch bodies, burst flashes, stars and fading tails. Expired sources contribute zero with no extra delayed light.

The old global two-light merge could put disjoint low and high events at a remote intensity centroid. Its enclosing cloud radius was also used as an attenuation denominator by custom receiver shading, unlike native Three point lights. This suppressed and distorted island illumination. The 16 ground-receiver proxies now keep rmsRadius/maxExtent as metadata, use a 0.01 m² punctual near-distance clamp on the matched native/custom paths, and preserve the distant 4096 m cutoff. They remain punctual spatial approximations, not finite-area emission.

**Water and aerial scattering take a separate six-record path** in production main.js: the same four non-firework sources plus at most two aggregates from getLightClusters(), rather than the 16 ground-receiver proxies. Those two clusters merge complete events, conserving instantaneous candela and summed RGB while losing spatial detail. water.js uses them in local seabed/refraction and foam terms; its explicit local specular term is the flashlight. aerial-perspective.js uses them for local air scattering. Clouds receive the two-cluster stream separately. Ground-receiver accuracy does not establish equal water/air spatial accuracy.

The default smoke shader borrows all 20 shared source records. With resolved smoke lighting active, it directly loops over only the first four non-firework records, substitutes an actual-source resolved field for the firework proxy contribution, and disables its legacy cluster loop to avoid double counting. The 20 smoke records are not 20 native scene lights.

The receiver builder caches its default result within a physical generation. Optional `getReceiverLightClusters({eventIds:[...]})` filters the same real luminous records before the same subdivision; it does not create synthetic lights or modify the default cache. Returned nonempty clusters and their position/RGB/ID arrays are frozen and remain structured-cloneable.

## CPU evidence and limits

`receiver-lifecycle-cpu-check.json` reports **7/8 checks passed**, `corePassed:true`, `pressurePassed:false` and overall `passed:false`. Four ordinary shows use 18 sampled phases, 35 base probes plus 255 independent probes on actual terrain and in foliage, with upward and four vertical normals. Maximum **held-out** RGB irradiance errors are 1.98% for six mixed events, 1.47% for opposed bursts, 1.72% for staggered launch/burst/tail overlap and 1.68% for silver waterfall plus low sweeping comets. Current source I, RGB and centroid remain conserved, filtered calls preserve the default cache, native/custom source attenuation agrees, and complete expiry leaves zero firework records.

The additional 12-event, all-azimuth near-field stress case fails its spatial criterion: at the worst low-source/sand-tongue phase, error is **9.10%** across the 255 held-out probes and **26.62%** across the 35 base probes. The former two-proxy held-out error in this phase was **93.13%**. Sixteen punctual proxies do not resolve arbitrary near-field source intersections. This is not an all-scenarios spatial-accuracy pass.

`receiver-temporal-integrity.json` passes **3/3** CPU checks. All 20 native firework families plus four vector profiles retain the before-change physical emission ledger and cloud/smoke clusters over 168 stages. Complete 30 Hz time series cover 631 six-event frames and 841 staggered frames. Maximum absolute spatial error is **2.11%** of peak reference irradiance; excess frame-to-frame change is at most **2.05%** of peak. Both traces reach zero after expiry. This checks sampled absolute proxy flicker and extinction, not derivative accuracy in near-dark phases or final tone-mapped motion.

CPU probes omit occlusion, smoke transmission, final tone mapping and pixels. Their held-out errors characterize the measured scenarios, not every allowed show or receiver position. The exploratory two/four/eight-light, angular-bin and tree reports and `before/` source snapshots remain diagnostics rather than production acceptance evidence.

## GPU pixels and remaining acceptance

`multi-receiver-scene.json` reports **41/41 checks passed** in an actual same-time production scene at sampled launch, ascent, before-burst, burst, overlap, mid-burn, late-burn and extinguished states. Opaque pre-temporal RGBA16F captures were read into RGBA32F and normalized for storage exposure. Both red and blue events have individually measured contributions on sand, vegetation and water in the combined scene; the measured source-position range is **154.94 m** and colour range **0.774**. At final extinction the firework records are zero, with no GL errors and temporary hooks restored. This is sampled GPU-pixel evidence of simultaneous illumination on those receivers, not calibrated lux or a visual-naturalness verdict.

The fixed-proxy superposition comparison keeps combined-scene positions, radii and colours fixed when isolating each contribution. Vegetation's residual is **0.184%** of its signal and resolves above FP16 readback uncertainty. Sand's **4.42%** and water's **313%** relative residuals are each *below the measured FP16 rounding bound* (water's signal is especially small). Those two tests pass only as **within quantization uncertainty**: they cannot establish exact linear addition at sand or water. Independently recompressing A and B before summing them is a different spatial approximation and is not claimed to equal normal AB compression exactly.

`natural-multi-lifecycle.json` passes **6/6 checks** over **785 actually rendered frames** for two events: launch/ascent, burst and late burn are covered without an all-dark live interval; emission centroid travel is **155.29 m**; terminal live emission falls below **0.01% of peak** before all firework records reach zero. This supports rendered-frame lifecycle continuity, while unrendered sub-frame intervals and subjective transition quality remain outside its scope.

`current-budget-check.json` passes **10/10** current-factory/source-contract checks but explicitly has **no GPU execution**. `smoke-compatibility.json` passes **8/8** isolated browser/GPU compatibility checks, including default 20-record smoke, resolved-field supplement, last-slot transient indirect and zero GL/console errors. Its one-pixel/one-packet fixtures do not establish full-island smoke appearance or performance. The recorded hashes for main.js, fireworks.js, firework-receiver-lighting.js, local-lights.js, water.js, pyro-smoke-renderer.js and pyro-smoke-light-field.js matched their current production files during this review.

The final `multi-previews.json` reports **both clips passed** (`passed:true`, `stage:complete`): island-light and multi-fireworks each cover both complete events through 0.7 s post-roll, capture all five stage images, schedule the production primary/body/crackle/ascent audio layers and two distinct attacks, and report no stale/voice-cap drops or GL errors. The final 960 × 720 isolated Chrome medium-SDR clips are 19.68 s and 19.48 s. `media-check.json` additionally passes **2/2** full-file and independent libvpx decode, payload-hash preservation after remux, indexed durations, Opus continuity and source-hash checks. A prior second capture missed the 0.5 s physical-versus-wall-clock pacing criterion by 0.052 s and is archived under `failed-event-clock-drift-media/`; the final captures are within 0.316 s and 0.013 s. The separate Opus and compositor clocks have approximate rather than sample-exact synchronization.

Final-frame review finds the large isolated white burst core greatly reduced at +0.094 s, while island light remains within about 0.35% of its prior regional brightness. The sand and nearshore change through pink, cyan-blue and gold and then dim. Early flower expansion still briefly forms a rounded bright region. This visual judgment used stills and sampled video frames, not calibrated lighting or full-rate subjective playback. The comparison page is a temporary development aid, not the product deliverable; the main scene, subjective listening and sustained application FPS remain distinct acceptance limits.
